HOW AUSTRALIA MOVES

Five decades of public transport across Australia's capital cities

Australia's capital cities do not move in the same way. This visualisation compares where public transport use is highest, how usage changes after population is considered, how different modes shape each city's transport system, and how patronage has changed over time.

02 — EVERY CITY MOVES DIFFERENTLY

Public transport systems are structured differently across Australia. Some cities rely strongly on rail, while others depend much more heavily on buses. Looking at mode dependence reveals another layer of difference between the capitals.

This graduated symbol map groups capitals into four bus-dependence classes based on the percentage of public transport trips carried by buses in 2024–25. Larger symbols indicate a higher bus share.

Reading the map: Symbol sizes represent discrete bus-share classes rather than exact proportional values. Larger circles indicate greater dependence on buses.

04 — THE COVID SHOCK AND RECOVERY

The COVID-19 pandemic created the largest disruption visible in Australia's recent public transport history. Patronage collapsed rapidly before beginning a multi-year recovery, but that recovery has not occurred equally across every capital.

NATIONAL SHOCK

The collapse — and the climb back

This waterfall separates the national change in capital-city patronage into annual losses and gains. Orange bars indicate year-on-year declines, while teal bars indicate recovery.

Key takeaway: The pandemic produced an unusually sharp patronage loss, followed by several consecutive years of recovery.

Each city's 2018–19 patronage is indexed to 100. Values above 100 indicate that 2024–25 patronage has exceeded the pre-COVID benchmark, while values below 100 indicate that recovery remains incomplete.

Reading the chart: The vertical benchmark at 100 represents full recovery to each city's own 2018–19 patronage level.

CONCLUSION

One country, eight different transport stories

Australia's public transport system cannot be understood through a single national pattern. Sydney and Melbourne dominate total patronage, but population-adjusted comparisons reveal a different hierarchy. Transport mode also matters: some capitals rely heavily on rail, while others remain overwhelmingly bus-oriented.

Across almost five decades, patronage generally expanded before the COVID-19 pandemic created an unprecedented break in the trend. The recovery since then has been substantial, but uneven — showing that Australia's capital-city transport systems continue to follow distinct trajectories.
