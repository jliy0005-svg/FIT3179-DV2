FIT3179 DATA VISUALISATION 2

HOW AUSTRALIA MOVES

Five decades of public transport across Australia's capital cities

Public transport works differently across Australia's capital cities. This visualisation looks at where public transport use is highest, how the picture changes when population is taken into account, how cities rely on different transport modes, and how COVID-19 disrupted long-term patronage trends.

01. WHERE AUSTRALIA MOVES

Public transport use looks very different across Australia's capital cities. Total patronage shows the size of each market, but population and transport mode also affect how those differences should be read.

TOTAL PATRONAGE

Australia's biggest public transport markets

Circle area represents total public transport passenger trips across Australia's capital cities in 2024–25. The map shows where patronage is most concentrated.

KEY INSIGHT Sydney and Melbourne dominate total patronage. Together, the two cities account for about 74% of all capital-city public transport trips in 2024–25.

CITY RANKING

Which capitals carry the most passengers?

Ranking the same 2024–25 figures makes the gap between Australia's largest and smaller public transport markets easier to compare.

BUS TRIPS PER RESIDENT

Where is bus travel most intensive?

Total bus patronage is strongly affected by population size. This dot map instead compares bus trips per resident. Each dot represents about five annual bus trips per resident in 2024–25.

KEY INSIGHT Sydney records the highest bus use relative to population. At around 52 bus trips per resident, its rate is more than twice Melbourne's approximately 22 trips per resident.
How to read the map: More dots indicate more bus trips per resident. Dot positions are schematic clusters around each capital and do not represent individual passengers, bus stops, routes or exact trip locations.

OVERALL USE PER RESIDENT

Which capitals use public transport most intensively overall?

This chart compares total public transport trips per resident across all modes. The dashed line shows the average across the eight capitals.

KEY INSIGHT The average is about 59.5 trips per resident. Sydney, Melbourne and Perth sit above this level, while the other five capitals fall below it.

02. EVERY CITY MOVES DIFFERENTLY

Each capital city has a different transport system and relies on different modes. Some cities have a mix of trains, trams, buses and ferries, while others depend mainly on buses.

BUS DEPENDENCE

How dependent is each city on buses?

This graduated symbol map groups capitals into four classes based on the percentage of public transport trips carried by buses. Larger symbols represent a higher bus share.

Important distinction: Chart 3 measures bus trips relative to population. Chart 5 measures how much of each city's public transport system depends on buses.

TRANSPORT MIX

Every city has a different transport mix

This heatmap compares the percentage of patronage carried by trains, trams and light rail, buses and ferries. Darker cells represent a larger share of the city's total trips.

KEY INSIGHT Mode dependence differs sharply between capitals. Buses account for all reported public transport patronage in Darwin and about 96% in Hobart, while larger systems such as Melbourne spread patronage across several modes.

NATIONAL MODE SHARE

Trains and buses carry most public transport trips

Across all eight capitals, trains and buses account for most public transport patronage. Each square represents about one percentage point of passenger trips in 2024–25.

Key takeaway: Trains account for about 43% and buses about 42% of capital-city public transport trips. Together, the two modes carry roughly 84% of all patronage.

03. FIVE DECADES ON THE MOVE

Public transport use has changed a lot over the last five decades. Most cities show long-term growth, but the trend was interrupted sharply during the COVID-19 period.

LONG-TERM CHANGE

Public transport trends from 1976–77 to 2024–25

Select a capital city to see how patronage has changed over time. Drag across the smaller overview chart to focus on a shorter period.

How to explore: Choose a capital from the dropdown, then drag across the lower timeline to zoom into a shorter period. The COVID-19 period creates a clear break in the longer-term trend.

CHANGING RANKS

How have city rankings changed over time?

This chart tracks each capital's position relative to the other cities over time. Rank 1 represents the largest public transport market in that financial year.

How to explore: Select a capital from the dropdown to highlight its ranking trajectory while keeping the other cities visible for comparison.

04. THE COVID SHOCK AND RECOVERY

COVID-19 caused the biggest disruption in recent public transport history. Patronage fell sharply before beginning a multi-year recovery, but not every capital has recovered at the same rate.

NATIONAL SHOCK

The collapse and recovery

This waterfall chart separates year-on-year changes in national capital-city patronage. Orange bars show declines and teal bars show increases.

COVID SHOCK The pandemic created the sharpest break in the series. Large consecutive losses were followed by several years of strong patronage growth as passengers returned to public transport.

RECOVERY BY CITY

Not every capital has returned to its pre-COVID level

Each city's 2018–19 patronage is indexed to 100. Values above 100 mean 2024–25 patronage has exceeded the pre-COVID benchmark, while values below 100 show that recovery is not complete.

RECOVERY CHECK Recovery has been uneven across the eight capitals. Perth has moved above its pre-COVID level at about 105, Brisbane is slightly above 100, while Canberra is very close to full recovery.
Reading the chart: The vertical benchmark at 100 represents each city's own 2018–19 patronage level.

CONCLUSION

One country, eight different transport stories

Australia's capital cities do not follow one single public transport pattern. Sydney and Melbourne dominate total patronage, but population-adjusted measures show a different picture of transport intensity. Cities also rely on very different combinations of trains, trams, buses and ferries.

Over the longer term, patronage generally increased before COVID-19 caused a major break in the trend. Recovery has been strong, but it remains uneven across the eight capitals.

Data coverage

Public transport patronage is shown for Australia's eight capital cities. Historical data extends from 1976–77 to 2024–25 where available.

View Vega Datasets source

Derived measures

Trips per resident combines BITRE passenger-trip totals with ABS Estimated Resident Population. Passenger-trip totals reported in millions were converted to individual trips before being divided by population.

Mode share is the percentage of reported public transport trips carried by each transport mode within a capital city.

Bus trips per resident combines BITRE bus patronage with ABS population estimates. In the dot map, each dot represents about five annual bus trips per resident.

Recovery index compares 2024–25 patronage with 2018–19 patronage. A value of 100 means the city has returned to its pre-COVID patronage level.

Data notes

Missing and not-applicable values in the original data were not automatically treated as zero.

Capital-city population uses the Greater Capital City Statistical Area definition from the Australian Bureau of Statistics.

Dot locations in Chart 3 are schematic clusters around capital-city coordinates and do not represent exact passenger origins, destinations, bus stops or routes.

Technical implementation

The visualisations were created using Vega-Lite and embedded in a single-page HTML and CSS website hosted on GitHub Pages. Interactive features include tooltips, dropdown filters and an overview-and-detail interval selection.

The three geographic visualisations use a proportional symbol map, a dot plot map and a graduated symbol map.
