HOW AUSTRALIA MOVES

Five decades of public transport across Australia's capital cities

Australia's capital cities do not move in the same way. This visualisation explores where public transport use is concentrated, how usage changes when population is considered, how cities differ in their reliance on buses, trains, trams and ferries, and how almost five decades of patronage were disrupted by COVID-19.

01 — WHERE AUSTRALIA MOVES

Public transport patronage varies substantially between Australia's capital cities. Raw totals reveal the largest markets, but population and transport mode can change how those differences are understood.

TOTAL PATRONAGE

Australia's biggest public transport markets

Circle area represents total public transport passenger trips across Australia's capital cities in 2024–25. This proportional symbol map highlights the concentration of patronage in Australia's largest urban transport markets.

CITY RANKING

Which capitals carry the most passengers?

Ranking the same 2024–25 patronage figures makes the differences between Australia's largest and smaller public transport markets easier to compare precisely.

BUS TRIPS PER RESIDENT

Where is bus travel most intensive?

Total bus patronage is strongly influenced by population size. This dot map instead compares bus trips relative to each capital's population. Each dot represents approximately five annual bus trips per resident in 2024–25.

How to read the map: More dots indicate more bus trips per resident. Dot positions are schematic clusters around each capital and do not represent individual passengers, bus stops, routes or exact trip locations.

OVERALL USE PER RESIDENT

Which capitals use public transport most intensively overall?

Looking beyond buses, this ranking compares total public transport trips per resident across all modes. The dashed reference line shows the average across the eight capitals.

02 — EVERY CITY MOVES DIFFERENTLY

Public transport systems are structured differently across Australia. Some cities combine rail, tram, bus and ferry networks, while others rely overwhelmingly on one mode. These differences shape how each capital moves.

BUS DEPENDENCE

Which transport systems depend most heavily on buses?

This graduated symbol map groups capitals into four classes according to the percentage of their public transport trips carried by buses. Larger symbols represent a higher bus share.

Important distinction: Chart 3 measures how many bus trips occur relative to population. Chart 5 instead measures how much of each city's entire public transport system depends on buses.

TRANSPORT MIX

Every city has a different transport mix

Bus dependence tells only part of the story. This heatmap compares the percentage of each city's public transport patronage carried by trains, trams and light rail, buses and ferries. Darker cells indicate a larger share of that city's total trips.

NATIONAL MODE SHARE

Trains and buses carry most public transport trips

Looking across all eight capitals, trains and buses account for most public transport patronage. Each square represents approximately one percentage point of passenger trips in 2024–25.

Key takeaway: Train and bus travel together account for the large majority of capital-city public transport patronage.

03 — FIVE DECADES ON THE MOVE

Public transport use has changed substantially over almost five decades. Long-term growth has been interrupted by periods of stagnation and, most dramatically, the COVID-19 disruption.

LONG-TERM CHANGE

Nearly five decades of growth, disruption and recovery

Select a capital city to explore how public transport patronage has changed since 1976–77. Drag across the smaller overview chart to focus on a particular period while preserving the full historical context.

How to explore: Choose a capital from the dropdown, then drag across the lower timeline to zoom into a shorter period.

CHANGING RANKS

Which capitals moved up — and which fell behind?

Absolute patronage tells only part of the long-term story. This ranking view tracks each capital's position relative to the other cities over time. Rank #1 represents the largest public transport market in that financial year.

How to explore: Select a capital from the dropdown to highlight its ranking trajectory while the other cities remain visible for context.

04 — THE COVID SHOCK AND RECOVERY

The COVID-19 pandemic created the largest disruption visible in Australia's recent public transport history. Patronage collapsed rapidly before beginning a multi-year recovery, but that recovery has not occurred equally across every capital.

NATIONAL SHOCK

The collapse — and the climb back

This waterfall separates national capital-city patronage change into annual losses and gains. Orange bars indicate year-on-year declines, while teal bars indicate recovery.

Key takeaway: The pandemic produced an unusually sharp patronage loss, followed by several consecutive years of recovery.

RECOVERY BY CITY

Not every capital has returned to its pre-COVID level

Each city's 2018–19 patronage is indexed to 100. Values above 100 indicate that 2024–25 patronage has exceeded the pre-COVID benchmark, while values below 100 indicate that recovery remains incomplete.

Reading the chart: The vertical benchmark at 100 represents full recovery to each city's own 2018–19 patronage level.

CONCLUSION

One country, eight different transport stories

Australia's public transport system cannot be understood through a single national pattern. Sydney and Melbourne dominate total patronage, but population-adjusted measures reveal different patterns of transport intensity. Mode choice also varies substantially: some capitals operate diverse multimodal networks, while others depend strongly on buses.

Across almost five decades, patronage generally expanded before the COVID-19 pandemic created an unprecedented break in the trend. Recovery has since been substantial but uneven, demonstrating that Australia's capital-city transport systems continue to follow distinct trajectories.

Derived measures

Trips per resident combines BITRE passenger-trip totals with ABS Estimated Resident Population. Passenger-trip totals reported in millions were converted to individual trips before being divided by population.

Mode share represents the proportion of reported public transport passenger trips carried by a particular transport mode within each capital city.

Recovery index compares 2024–25 patronage with 2018–19 patronage, where 100 represents a return to the city's pre-COVID level.

Chart 3 dot map: each dot represents approximately five annual bus trips per resident. Dots are positioned schematically around capital-city coordinates and do not indicate actual passenger origins, destinations, routes or stops.
